## Refined framing used from Top 100 stage onward (post-mentorship, same idea, sharper focus)

After Top 100 selection and FITT–IIT Delhi mentorship, the pitch was sharpened around one flagship, fully-working module — a cricket bowling-action legality checker against the ICC's real 15° elbow-extension rule (ICC Law 24.3) — rather than the broader multi-sport framing filed at Phase 1. The underlying idea, theme, and technology (MediaPipe + on-device joint-angle analysis) are unchanged; only the demonstrated scope narrowed to what could be built as a genuine, working prototype.

**Refined problem statement:**
> 98% of rural Indian athletes train with zero access to biomechanical analysis — coaches can't detect unsafe or illegal technique (e.g. bowling actions, joint overload) without labs costing ₹5,000+/hour, leading to preventable injuries, disqualified talent, and missed scouting opportunities.

**Refined solution statement:**
> DrishtiSport AI: a free, 100% offline smartphone app that turns any budget camera into a biomechanics coach. Using on-device computer vision (MediaPipe + quantized ONNX), it tracks joint angles in real time — starting with cricket bowling actions against the ICC's 15° elbow-extension legality rule — flagging unsafe or illegal technique instantly via local-language audio/visual alerts, and logging a tamper-proof, data-backed performance record for coaches and scouts.

**Refined target users:**
> 1) Young cricketers and athletes (10–22) in Tier 2/3 and rural India training without access to biomechanics coaching. 2) PE teachers, local coaches, and umpires who need an objective way to flag unsafe or illegal technique without expensive equipment. 3) Talent scouts who need verifiable, bias-free performance data instead of subjective local judgment.
