## 1. Program Structure (Confirmed via Web Research)

- **Organizers**: Samsung India, in partnership with FITT (IIT Delhi), MeitY, and UN in India.
- **2026 is the 5th edition.** Grants doubled this year to ₹2 crore total (up from ₹1 crore in 2025).
- **Eligibility**: Indian nationals, 14–22 years, teams up to 5.
- **Four themes, one applies to exactly one**: AI Living for India, Health & Education, Environmental Sustainability, and **Sport & Tech** (called "Social Change through Sport and Tech" in 2025 materials).
- **Rule**: Applying to multiple themes risks disqualification. You cannot "mix" themes — confirms our answer above.

### Stage funnel
1. **Application (closed)** → screened by FITT-IIT Delhi subject experts on: relevance to a real social problem, technical feasibility, market potential, team competence.
2. **Top 100** (25 per theme) — *you are here.* Online training: design thinking, digital masterclasses, mentorship (starting ~July 2026).
3. **Top 40** (10 per theme) — prototype grant (₹20k/team) + Samsung laptops. Mandatory Samsung site visits + residential bootcamp at IIT Delhi (~September 2026).
4. **Top 20** (5 per theme) — bigger grant (₹1L/team) + Galaxy Z Flip devices. Training on innovation, entrepreneurship, prototyping, IPR. Grand Finale: 2-day pitch to a grand jury in Delhi NCR, investor meets.
5. **Grand Finale winners**: **exactly 4 teams — one per theme** — split ₹2 crore + 6-month FITT-IIT Delhi incubation.
   - Plus 2 Community Choice Awards (₹2L total) and 1 Digital Impact Award (₹50k) — these are separate, public-vote/engagement-based, open to more teams.

### Judging criteria (as stated by the program)
1. Innovation & creativity — uniqueness/originality
2. Impact & feasibility — does it solve a real problem, can it actually be built
3. Scalability — can it reach beyond the pilot group
4. Technical & execution capability — clarity of implementation

### ⚠️ Critical finding: last year's Sport & Tech winner is very close to your concept

**NextPlay.AI (2025 Sport & Tech winner, ₹1 crore + IIT Delhi incubation):**
- Founders: two 21-year-old tennis players / CS students (IIIT Pune), from Aurangabad.
- **What it does**: Analyzes tennis videos with ML to give personalized feedback on strokes, footwork, match movement — automated objective performance metrics from video (+ optional wearable data).
- **Positioning**: "Democratising tennis coaching for grassroots athletes" — explicitly targets Tier 2/3 athletes who can't afford academies/elite coaches.
- Broader platform pitch: AI Virtual Coach + AI Referee (fairness in officiating) + Neuro-Inclusive Tracker (inclusion for differently-abled athletes).

This is **the same core thesis as DrishtiSport AI**: video/pose-based AI coaching, democratizing access for Tier 2/3 grassroots athletes, closing the gap left by expensive human coaching. Judges in your exact theme rewarded this pattern *last year*. That's double-edged:
- **Good news**: it validates that judges respond strongly to this problem framing — you're not chasing an unproven thesis.
- **Real risk**: if your pitch reads as "the same idea, different sport," a jury that saw NextPlay.AI win will discount it as derivative. You need visible, defensible differentiation, not just a different sport list.

**Where DrishtiSport AI can differentiate from NextPlay.AI (don't change the core idea — sharpen these angles):**
1. **Injury/biomechanical-safety framing, not just coaching.** NextPlay pitched coaching + fairness + inclusion. Your unique wedge is **real-time injury-risk detection** (unsafe joint angle thresholds, audio alerts) — a genuinely different value prop (safety, not just skill improvement).
2. **Anti-corruption / tamper-proof scouting angle.** This is not something NextPlay's public materials emphasize. Your "verified digital performance log that bypasses local selection bias" angle is a distinct, judge-resonant narrative (ties into transparency/UN partnership themes).
3. **Multi-sport, single-body-mechanics-engine approach** (cricket, sprinting, kabaddi) vs. NextPlay's single-sport (tennis) deep dive — lead with this as breadth, but back it with one flawless working demo (per your own Section 8 "don't overcomplicate scope" rule — pick 1 sport for the MVP demo, mention multi-sport as the roadmap).
4. **Explicitly reference on-device/offline/edge-AI performance** — Samsung's own hardware/NPU angle — harder than NextPlay's cloud-based video analysis model (if NextPlay's is cloud-based; this needs verifying live, but "zero cloud dependency" is a stronger Samsung-hardware showcase either way).

## 5. FINALIZED STRATEGY (Locked — Build From This)

**Decision**: Continue with Sport & Tech + DrishtiSport AI. No theme mixing, no idea pivot. Differentiate from NextPlay.AI via injury-safety + anti-corruption framing, and **narrow the MVP to one sport that must be a real, working, demoable thing at every remaining stage** (Top 40 site visit, Top 20 bootcamp, Grand Finale pitch — all require a live/working prototype, not slides).

### 5.1 The MVP sport: Cricket fast-bowling action (not squats, not generic fitness)

Chosen over squat/sprint/kabaddi for one reason: **it maps to a real, existing biomechanical rule that Indian cricket already enforces** — the ICC's **15° elbow extension limit** for a legal bowling action (beyond this, an action is called "chucking" and is illegal). This gives you:
- A concrete, citable, judge-defensible threshold (not an invented "safe angle") — direct technical credibility.
- A direct tie to your own problem statement: local umpires/coaches in rural India have no way to check this without a ₹5,000/hr biomechanics lab — DrishtiSport AI puts that same check in a phone.
- Total separation from NextPlay.AI (tennis stroke coaching) — no overlap in sport, in threshold, or in framing.
- A single, narrow, fully-buildable scope for the demo (per your own "don't overcomplicate scope" rule) — sprint/squat/kabaddi become the *roadmap slide*, not the MVP.

**Demo build (must actually run, not be faked — judges catch faked animations immediately):**
1. Python + OpenCV + MediaPipe Pose script, run locally (no cloud call).
2. Input: a 5–10 sec sample clip of a bowling action (record your own or use a public practice clip).
3. Extract elbow joint angle (shoulder–elbow–wrist landmarks) frame-by-frame during the delivery stride.
4. Overlay: **green skeleton** while elbow extension stays under 15°, **red skeleton + on-screen warning** ("Illegal action risk — elbow extension 19°") the instant it crosses threshold.
5. Log the max angle reached + a simple pass/fail "legal action" verdict to a local SQLite row — this becomes your "talent score / verified log" proof-of-concept in miniature.
6. Secondary angle (only if time permits, not required for MVP): knee flexion at front-foot landing, for generic injury-risk flagging — keep this as a "roadmap" bullet, don't build it for the first demo.

This is the one thing to build first. Everything else (Flutter app shell, multi-sport support, cloud sync, local-language voice) stays roadmap until this core loop is solid and demoable end-to-end.

### 5.2 Finalized application/pitch text (updated framing, same char limits)

**Field 12 — Problem (300 char limit):**
> 98% of rural Indian athletes train without access to biomechanical analysis — coaches can't detect unsafe or illegal technique (e.g. bowling actions, joint overload) without labs costing ₹5,000+/hour, leading to preventable injuries, disqualified talent, and missed scouting opportunities.

**Field 13 — Solution (500 char limit):**
> DrishtiSport AI: a free, 100% offline smartphone app that turns any budget camera into a biomechanics coach. Using on-device computer vision (MediaPipe + quantized ONNX), it tracks joint angles in real time — starting with cricket bowling actions against the ICC's 15° elbow-extension legality rule — flagging unsafe or illegal technique instantly via local-language audio/visual alerts, and logging a tamper-proof, data-backed performance record for coaches and scouts.

**Field 14 — Target users (500 char limit):**
> 1) Young cricketers and athletes (10–22) in Tier 2/3 and rural India training without access to biomechanics coaching. 2) PE teachers, local coaches, and umpires who need an objective way to flag unsafe or illegal technique without expensive equipment. 3) Talent scouts who need verifiable, bias-free performance data instead of subjective local judgment.

### 5.3 Finalized 7-slide deck copy

1. **Problem**: "A ₹5,000/hour biomechanics lab decides whether a bowling action is legal. A rural academy has no way to check it at all." (split visual: lab vs. mud ground/local nets)
2. **Impact**: Talented bowlers called for "chucking" with no warning until a real match; injuries from undetected unsafe technique end careers early — quantify with a simple stat/chart on injury dropout rates.
3. **Current Gaps**: Wearable sensors and pro motion-capture systems are expensive, lab-bound, and inaccessible to grassroots coaches; existing apps are manual/subjective.
4. **Solution**: Live screen recording — phone camera → real-time skeleton overlay → green/red elbow-angle flag against the ICC 15° rule, audio alert in local language.
5. **Architecture**: Camera feed → MediaPipe landmark extraction → on-device ONNX angle/classification model → visual+audio output, 100% offline, runs on sub-₹10,000 phones.
6. **Scalability & Business Model**: Free for athletes/schools; B2B subscription for academies; expansion roadmap to sprint/kabaddi/squat injury-risk modules; talent-scouting analytics dashboard for sports bodies.
7. **Team & IIT Delhi Plan**: Team credentials + explicit ask to use FITT–IIT Delhi labs for camera-parallax calibration and validating the elbow-angle model against real biomechanics equipment.

### 5.4 What must be true at each remaining stage (reality check, not just slides)

- **Top 40 site visit / bootcamp (~Sept 2026)**: bring the working bowling-action demo above, running live on a laptop or phone — not a video recording of it, an actual live run judges can watch and even try.
- **Top 20 Grand Finale pitch**: same core demo, polished — ideally running natively on an Android phone (Flutter shell or even a simple Kivy/Chaquopy wrapper around the Python core) rather than a laptop script, since judges will expect to see it as "the product," not "the research code."
- **Do not promise multi-sport, cloud dashboard, or scouting marketplace as if built** — those stay clearly labeled roadmap/future-scale items on slide 6, never demoed as if working, per your own "no half-finished implementations" rule and the competition's "don't fake the AI" warning.
